## The six, in more depth

### 1. Taco Reef — Mexican / Tex-Mex
Tacos and burritos are about as globally familiar as food gets — Russian, German, Italian, and British guests all know the format on sight, and it travels well in a delivery bag. It's also an easy brand to make look vibrant. **Kitchen-fit:** reuses the same wrap-and-fill station and bread-handling skill you're already building for Shawrimp — same motion, different fillings, no new equipment category. Signature idea: a smoky braised chicken or beef taco with chipotle crema and pickled onion — kept distinct from Shawrimp's shrimp so the two don't compete for the same "signature seafood" hook.

### 2. Yamas — Greek
Gyro/souvlaki sits close enough to shawarma to feel familiar to Egyptian customers, while still reading as a distinct "European" choice for European tourists — an easy sell to the same German/Italian/Russian mix already driving your seafood and Italian demand. **Kitchen-fit:** a rotisserie serving Shawrimp in one daypart could plausibly serve gyro in another — real equipment-sharing logic, though shellfish/meat cross-contact on a shared spit is a genuine food-safety question to check with an actual kitchen consultant, not a detail to wave away. Signature idea: a rotisserie gyro plate — chicken or beef, tzatziki, Greek salad, warm pita, lemon potatoes.

### 3. Reef Bowl — Poke / Hawaiian-style bowls
"Healthy" cafes already do fine in Sharm (Ju & Co, Flamango, Bus Stop are all live and "Healthy"-labeled), so the appetite is proven — nobody's doing a dedicated poke/bowl format yet. More importantly, it's the one idea here that's operationally *different*, not just menu-different: cold assembly, no stove, no fryer. Every one of your current six brands is hot, fried, or baked; this is the one that spreads load across kitchen stations instead of stacking everyone onto the same grill/fryer at peak. **Kitchen-fit:** raw or lightly-cured fish needs the same proven cold chain that gates Shawrimp — the honest sequencing is to launch with cooked-protein bowls (chicken, shrimp, tofu) first and add raw tuna/salmon only once that cold chain is demonstrated, not on day one. Signature idea: seared tuna or shrimp, sushi rice, edamame, mango, avocado, spicy mayo.

### 4. Tiffin — Indian / curry
Curry travels unusually well in delivery — it's sauce-based, so it doesn't dry out or go soggy the way fried food does over a 20-minute ride. Modern, not-too-fiery Indian food also has broad "familiar adventurous" appeal with European tourists. **Kitchen-fit:** batch-cooked curry bases ladled to order over rice is exactly the "made ahead, plated fast" model that keeps Sukkar's live-line load near zero — not a new operating skill, just a new recipe category. Signature idea: a butter chicken box — basmati rice, garlic naan, cucumber raita.

### 5. Ember — Western BBQ / smokehouse
Egyptian grill is everywhere in Sharm; genuine low-and-slow Western BBQ (ribs, brisket, pulled meat) is nowhere. It's distinct from both Sinai Smash and every Egyptian grill on the platform, and "smoked meat" is a strong, visual marketing hook. **The honest caveat:** real BBQ needs hours of low-temperature cooking and a dedicated smoker — the opposite of the "smallest ingredient set, most forgiving prep" logic that made Sinai Smash the deliberate first launch. Of the six ideas here, this is the one that doesn't fit the kitchen's current operating model without a workaround (e.g. pressure-braised "pulled" meat instead of a true overnight smoke — a different, lesser product than the name promises). Worth pressure-testing with an actual pitmaster before treating it as "just another menu."

### 6. Basil House — Vietnamese (pho, banh mi)
Thai, Chinese, and Japanese are all already live on the platform; Vietnamese isn't, and its fresh-herb, lighter profile is a real point of difference against the heavier Asian and Italian options already available. Banh mi is also a sandwich — tourists understand the format instantly, no explanation needed. **Kitchen-fit:** banh mi reuses the same bread-and-fill sandwich skill as Falafel Smash and Ta'meya; pho needs a long-simmered broth made in batches ahead of service, which is more forgiving than it sounds — prep-forward like Sukkar, not cooked to order. Signature idea: a five-spice chicken banh mi with pickled carrot, daikon, chili, and coriander.

## The ratio worth checking before you greenlight any of these

Right now you have 6 own-brand concepts on the table (the 5 in the plan plus Wingsstop) against 42 real restaurants live in the marketplace (45 rows minus the grocery merchant, the pharmacy, and the app-review demo entry). If all six ideas above were added on top of that, you'd be at 12 own brands against 42 third-party — roughly **22% of the visible catalog would be yours.** Your own plan calls 17% "looks astroturfed" and 8% "unremarkable." Gate #7 already requires ≥40 live third-party merchants before brand #2 even launches — so the more useful move is probably shortlisting one or two of these rather than all six, and letting the ratio improve as real partner count grows rather than the other way around.
